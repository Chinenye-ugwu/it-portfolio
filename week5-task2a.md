# Task 2A: Usability Testing and Design Enhancements

## Introduction

As part of Week 5, I conducted usability testing on my Professional Growth Portfolio to evaluate its navigation, content organization, readability, accessibility, and overall user experience.

I wanted to make sure that a visitor could use the portfolio easily and locate the information presented in each section. I tested the portfolio using both my mobile phone and laptop. Testing on different devices was important because visitors may access a professional portfolio from different screen sizes.

The testing process also helped me identify an issue that I had not noticed while developing the portfolio. This experience reinforced the importance of testing a website from the perspective of the user rather than assuming that everything is working correctly.


## Issue Identified: Contact Page Links

During the mobile usability test, I discovered an issue on the Contact page. The LinkedIn, GitHub, and portfolio addresses were displayed as plain text rather than clickable links.

Although the information was present, displaying the addresses as plain text created an unnecessary barrier for visitors. A user would have to manually copy the address or type it into a browser instead of simply selecting the link.

This was especially important because the Contact page is intended to make it easy for professionals, recruiters, mentors, instructors, and other visitors to connect with me.

The issue showed me that a page can contain the correct information and still have a usability problem. From a user's perspective, having a working link is more convenient and accessible than having to manually enter a web address.


## Design Enhancement Implemented

I corrected the Contact page issue by converting the three URLs into clickable Markdown links.

The Contact page now provides direct clickable links for:

- **LinkedIn Profile**
- **GitHub Profile**
- **Professional Portfolio**

This improvement reduced the number of steps required for a visitor to reach my professional profiles and portfolio.

The change was small, but it improved the functionality of an important part of the website. A visitor can now select the appropriate link instead of manually copying or typing the web address.

This experience also reminded me that usability improvements do not always require major changes to a website. Sometimes a small adjustment can make the experience significantly easier for the user.


## Retesting After the Fix

After making the correction, I committed the changes to my GitHub repository and tested the portfolio again using my laptop.

I selected each of the updated Contact-page links and confirmed that they were functioning correctly. The LinkedIn, GitHub, and portfolio links now open as intended.

I also reviewed the other portfolio sections again and confirmed that they displayed their appropriate content.

This gave me a complete usability-testing cycle:

**Test → Identify Issue → Implement Fix → Retest → Verify**

The issue was therefore not simply identified; it was corrected and tested again to confirm that the improvement worked.


## Navigation and Content Testing

In addition to the Contact page, I tested the other portfolio sections.

I checked the navigation throughout the portfolio and confirmed that the sections displayed their appropriate content. I also reviewed the IT497 weekly materials to make sure the available sections could be accessed through the portfolio navigation.

The testing confirmed that the portfolio structure was understandable and that visitors could access the intended information through the navigation.

The weekly organization also helps demonstrate my progression throughout IT497. Rather than presenting my work as unrelated assignments, the portfolio allows visitors to follow my development from one week to another.

Testing the navigation also helped me recognize the importance of maintaining consistency as additional weekly content is added.


## Accessibility and User Experience

The usability testing also helped me consider accessibility and the overall experience of visitors who may use different devices.

Testing on both a mobile phone and a laptop helped me recognize that a portfolio should not be designed only for the device used by the creator. Visitors may use phones, tablets, laptops, or desktop computers, so the content should remain understandable and usable across different screen sizes.

I also considered the readability of the portfolio, the organization of headings, the clarity of navigation, and the accessibility of important external links.

Making the Contact page links clickable was an important accessibility improvement because it reduced the effort required to reach my professional profiles.

These improvements support the purpose of my Professional Growth Portfolio by making my work easier for visitors to find, understand, and explore.


## Impact of the Enhancements

The usability testing and resulting improvements made the portfolio more user-friendly and professional.

The most significant issue I identified was the Contact page link problem. Correcting the links made it easier for visitors to connect with me and reduced unnecessary steps in the user experience.

Testing the portfolio on both a mobile phone and laptop also increased my confidence that the content and navigation were functioning appropriately across different devices.

The testing process helped me understand that usability is an ongoing process. A portfolio should not simply be created and left unchanged. It should be tested, reviewed, improved, and tested again as new content and features are added.

These improvements also support my professional goal because System Administrators and other IT professionals must think about technology from the user's perspective. A technically functional system is not enough if users cannot easily understand or interact with it.


## Reflection on the Testing Experience

This usability-testing experience taught me that creating a professional portfolio requires more than adding content. I also need to consider how another person will experience and interact with that content.

Initially, I was focused mainly on making sure the information was present. Testing the portfolio helped me shift my attention toward the visitor's experience. The Contact page issue was a good example of this. The information was technically present, but the way it was presented made it less convenient for the user.

Testing with my phone was especially helpful because it allowed me to experience the portfolio from a different perspective. I then used my laptop to verify the correction after making the necessary changes.

As I continue developing my IT skills, I want to carry this user-centered approach into my professional work. Whether I am managing a system, supporting users, or working with cloud technologies, I want to consider not only whether a technical solution works, but also whether it works well for the people who depend on it.


## Conclusion

The usability testing process helped me improve both the functionality and user experience of my Professional Growth Portfolio.

By testing the portfolio on my mobile phone and laptop, I was able to identify a problem that was not immediately obvious during development. The Contact page displayed my professional URLs as plain text instead of clickable links. I corrected the issue and retested the page on my laptop to confirm that the links were functioning properly.

I also reviewed the navigation and content throughout the portfolio and confirmed that the sections displayed their appropriate information.

This experience reinforced the importance of iterative design. Creating a website is not a one-time process. Effective design requires testing, identifying problems, making improvements, and testing again.

The improvements I made have made my portfolio easier to navigate and more convenient for visitors to use. More importantly, the experience has strengthened my understanding of user-centered design, accessibility, and the responsibility IT professionals have to create technology that works effectively for the people who use it.
