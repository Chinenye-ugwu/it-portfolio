## Task 1 – Integrating Christlike Attributes and Addressing Personal Warning Signs

### Introduction

As I continue developing my Professional Growth Portfolio, I have realized that professional development is not only about acquiring technical skills. It is also about becoming the kind of person who can use those skills responsibly and serve others with integrity. My education in Information Technology has challenged me academically, professionally, and personally. Learning cloud computing, Linux administration, networking, cybersecurity, and system administration has sometimes been difficult, but these challenges have helped me recognize qualities that I need to continue developing.

My faith has become an important part of this development. It reminds me that technical knowledge and professional success should be accompanied by patience, humility, integrity, compassion, responsibility, and a willingness to serve. My portfolio therefore represents more than completed assignments. It represents an ongoing journey of becoming a better student, a more capable IT professional, and a person whose actions reflect Christlike values.

### Christlike Attributes in My Personal and Professional Development

#### Patience

Patience is one of the Christlike attributes that has become especially important during my IT education. Technology does not always work as expected. A configuration may fail, a command may produce an unexpected result, or a lab may require several attempts before I understand what went wrong.

I have learned that becoming frustrated does not make a technical problem easier to solve. Instead, patience allows me to slow down, examine the problem carefully, research possible solutions, and try again. This has been particularly valuable when working with Azure configurations and Linux environments.

Patience is also important in professional relationships. As a future System Administrator or Cloud Support professional, I may work with users who have different levels of technical knowledge. I want to remember that a problem that seems simple to me may be very stressful or confusing to another person. Demonstrating patience allows me to provide support without making people feel embarrassed or ignored.

#### Humility and Willingness to Learn

My transition into Information Technology has reminded me that I do not know everything, and I do not need to pretend that I do. There are many technologies, commands, concepts, and tools that I am still learning.

Humility helps me recognize when I need to ask questions, review documentation, seek assistance, or try another approach. Instead of viewing mistakes as evidence that I cannot succeed in IT, I am learning to see them as opportunities to grow.

This connects strongly with my principle of continuous learning. Every difficult assignment has taught me something about both technology and myself. My goal is to remain teachable throughout my career rather than assuming that completing a degree means that learning is finished.

#### Integrity

Integrity is one of the most important values I want to carry into my IT career. System administrators and other technology professionals may have access to sensitive information, user accounts, organizational systems, and critical infrastructure. That access creates a responsibility to act honestly and carefully.

My faith teaches me that character matters even when other people are not watching. In my professional life, this means I should not misuse access privileges, disclose confidential information, ignore security concerns, or change systems without proper authorization.

Integrity also means being honest about mistakes. If I make an error that affects a system, I want to take responsibility, communicate appropriately, and work toward correcting the problem rather than hiding it. My portfolio should therefore demonstrate not only what I have accomplished but also the values that guide how I approach my work.

#### Service and Compassion

Service is another important connection between my faith and my professional goals. I chose Information Technology because I want to develop skills that allow me to solve problems and help people.

In a professional IT environment, service can take many forms. It may involve helping a user regain access to an account, troubleshooting a system problem, maintaining reliable infrastructure, protecting organizational data, or explaining a technical issue in language that a nontechnical person can understand.

I want to approach these responsibilities with compassion and respect. Technology should not make people feel powerless. A good IT professional should help users feel supported and confident while maintaining appropriate security and professional boundaries.

### Recognizing My Personal Warning Signs

One important part of spiritual growth is recognizing when I am beginning to move away from the qualities I want to demonstrate. I have learned that warning signs do not necessarily mean that I have failed. Instead, they can serve as signals that I need to pause, reflect, and realign myself.

One warning sign for me is **frustration when a technical problem does not work after several attempts**. When I have spent a long time troubleshooting an Azure, Linux, networking, or cybersecurity problem without success, I can become discouraged. If I continue working while frustrated, I may become impatient and stop thinking as carefully as I should.

Another warning sign is **feeling overwhelmed by multiple responsibilities**. Balancing coursework, assignments, technical labs, and other responsibilities can sometimes make me feel as though everything needs to be completed immediately. When I begin thinking this way, I recognize the need to slow down and prioritize rather than allowing stress to control my decisions.

A third warning sign is **doubting my ability to learn difficult technical concepts**. Because I am developing skills in a new professional field, there are moments when unfamiliar technologies can make me question whether I am capable of becoming the IT professional I hope to become.

A fourth warning sign is **isolating myself when I am struggling**. I have learned through prayer, fellowship, and other spiritual experiences that I do not have to face every challenge alone. When I stop communicating with others and try to carry every difficulty by myself, I can lose perspective and become more discouraged.

### Strategies for Realignment

Recognizing these warning signs is only useful if I respond to them appropriately. I have therefore identified several strategies that help me return to a healthier spiritual and professional mindset.

#### 1. Pause and Pray

When I recognize frustration or discouragement, I can pause instead of immediately reacting. Prayer and quiet reflection help me remember that one difficult assignment does not define my ability or my future.

Proverbs 3:5–6 reminds me to trust God rather than relying entirely on my own understanding. This helps me approach difficult situations with greater humility and patience.

#### 2. Break Difficult Problems Into Smaller Steps

When a technical problem feels overwhelming, I can divide it into smaller tasks. Instead of asking myself, "How am I going to fix everything?" I can ask, "What is the next thing I need to verify?"

This approach has helped me during technical labs because troubleshooting becomes a process rather than an emotional reaction. It also reinforces the professional habit of systematic problem-solving.

#### 3. Use Gospel Music and Quiet Time

Listening to gospel music while working on difficult labs has become a practical way for me to maintain peace and focus. When a lab does not work as expected, music can help me calm down and approach the problem one step at a time.

Quiet time also gives me an opportunity to reflect on God's mercies and recognize how far I have already come. These practices help me remember that my educational journey is part of a larger process of growth.

#### 4. Seek Help and Fellowship

When I am struggling, I should not interpret asking for help as weakness. Seeking clarification, reviewing documentation, asking classmates or instructors questions, and learning from others are all important parts of professional development.

My weekly prayer fellowship has also reminded me of the importance of community. Listening to other people's experiences and testimonies strengthens my faith and reminds me that challenges are easier to face when I am willing to connect with others.

#### 5. Return to My Core Principles

When I feel distracted or discouraged, I can return to the three guiding principles that have shaped my portfolio: **integrity, service, and continuous learning**.

Integrity reminds me to do what is right.

Service reminds me that my technical abilities should ultimately help others.

Continuous learning reminds me that not understanding something today does not mean I cannot learn it tomorrow.

These principles provide a practical framework for making decisions during both academic challenges and future professional situations.

### Integration Into My Professional Growth Portfolio

The integration of Christlike attributes into my portfolio is intentional. I do not want my spiritual growth and professional development to appear as two unrelated parts of my life. Instead, I want the portfolio to show how they influence one another.

My technical projects demonstrate my developing skills in areas such as cloud computing, system administration, networking, cybersecurity, and troubleshooting. My reflections explain the character qualities developed through those experiences.

### Reflection and Commitment

As I reflect on my spiritual and professional development, I recognize that I am still growing. I do not expect to respond perfectly to every challenge, but I want to become more aware of my reactions and more intentional about correcting them.

My goal is to become an IT professional who can be trusted with important systems and sensitive information while also treating people with patience, respect, fairness, and compassion. I want my technical work to reflect excellence, but I also want my character to be visible in the way I approach problems, interact with colleagues, support users, and make ethical decisions.

My faith gives me a foundation for this goal. Scriptures such as Proverbs 3:5–6, Doctrine and Covenants 58:26–28, and Philippians 4:13 have helped me understand the importance of trust, personal agency, perseverance, and reliance on God's strength. Ultimately, my Professional Growth Portfolio is a reflection of the person I am becoming. I want it to show that technical growth and spiritual growth can strengthen one another. As I continue preparing for a career in System Administration and Cloud Support, I am committed to developing not only my technical competence but also the patience, integrity, humility, service, and perseverance necessary to use those skills responsibly.

## Task 2B – Ethical Dilemma Reflection

### Introduction

Ethical decision-making is an important part of Information Technology because IT professionals often have access to information, systems, and resources that can significantly affect other people. Technical knowledge gives a professional the ability to perform many actions, but having the ability to do something does not necessarily mean that it is appropriate to do it.

The ethical dilemma I examined earlier in my IT497 portfolio involved an insurance manager who recorded a life insurance beneficiary and shared the video publicly without clear evidence that the individual had provided informed consent. Although the situation occurred in an insurance setting rather than an IT department, it helped me recognize ethical issues that are directly relevant to my future career in Information Technology.

The situation raised important questions about privacy, consent, professional responsibility, dignity, and the appropriate use of personal information.

### My View of the Ethical Dilemma

My view is that the individual's privacy and dignity should have been respected before the recording was shared publicly. Even if the intention behind the post was positive, good intentions do not automatically remove the need for consent.

A person may be comfortable participating in an insurance transaction but may not be comfortable having their image shared publicly. These are two different decisions. Consent to participate in one activity should not automatically be interpreted as consent to use someone's image or personal information for another purpose.

I believe the manager should have obtained clear permission before recording or publicly sharing the individual's image. If permission was not provided, the safest and most respectful decision would have been not to publish the video.

This situation reinforced for me that ethical behavior requires more than asking whether an action is possible or beneficial. It requires considering how the action affects the person involved.

### Connection to Information Technology

Although the original situation was not specifically an IT problem, the principles apply directly to Information Technology.

IT professionals regularly work with information that belongs to other people. This can include usernames, passwords, employee records, customer information, financial information, system logs, photographs, communications, and other forms of personal or organizational data.

Because of this access, an IT professional may sometimes have the technical ability to view, copy, change, or share information. However, technical access does not mean that the information can be used for any purpose.

For example, a system administrator may have administrative privileges that allow access to files belonging to employees. That privilege exists to perform legitimate job responsibilities. It does not give the administrator permission to browse personal files out of curiosity or disclose confidential information.

The ethical lesson I gained from the insurance dilemma is therefore directly applicable to system administration: access must be accompanied by responsibility.

### How I Would Handle a Similar Situation in Future Work

If I encounter a similar ethical situation in my future IT career, my first response would be to identify what information or activity is involved and determine whether I have legitimate authorization to access, use, or share it.

I would consider several questions:

- Do I have authorization to access this information?
- What is the legitimate business purpose?
- Has the person provided appropriate consent when consent is required?
- Could sharing or using the information cause harm?
- Does the proposed action follow organizational policy?
- Are there legal, regulatory, security, or privacy requirements that apply?
- Would I be comfortable explaining my decision to my supervisor or another appropriate authority?

If I determined that the action was inappropriate, I would not simply proceed because someone requested it. I would respectfully raise the concern and seek clarification from the appropriate supervisor, privacy officer, security professional, or organizational authority.

If the situation involved a serious privacy or security concern, I would follow the organization's established reporting and escalation procedures.

### The Importance of Speaking Up

One of the most important lessons I take from this dilemma is that ethical responsibility sometimes requires speaking up.

It can be difficult to question an action when the person requesting it has more authority or experience. However, professionalism does not mean agreeing with every decision made by someone in a higher position.

As an IT professional, I may encounter situations in which a supervisor, coworker, or client asks me to perform an action that concerns me. I want to respond respectfully while still protecting users, organizational information, and professional standards.

I would avoid responding emotionally or accusing someone of wrongdoing without understanding the situation. Instead, I would ask questions, identify the specific concern, refer to relevant policies or procedures, and seek guidance when necessary.

This approach allows me to protect both the organization and the people affected by the decision.

### Ethical Principles I Will Carry Into My Career

Several principles from this dilemma will guide my future professional decisions.

#### Privacy

Personal information should be treated carefully. I should access and use information only when there is a legitimate reason and appropriate authorization.

#### Consent

When consent is required, I should not assume that permission for one activity automatically applies to another activity.

#### Integrity

I should do what is right even when taking the ethical path is less convenient or when no one appears to be watching.

#### Accountability

I should accept responsibility for my actions and be willing to explain the decisions I make.

#### Respect for Human Dignity

Technology involves people. Behind every account, record, photograph, or data entry is a real person who deserves to be treated with respect.

#### Professional Responsibility

Technical skills should be used responsibly. An IT professional's ability to access a system creates an obligation to protect that system and the people whose information it contains.

### What This Experience Taught Me

This ethical dilemma helped me understand that ethical challenges in IT are not always obvious. They may appear as ordinary technical requests.

For example, someone might ask an administrator to retrieve information from an employee's account, provide access to a file, disable a security control, share a screenshot, or make a system change. The request may sound simple, but each situation can involve questions about authorization, privacy, security, and responsibility.

The experience has therefore changed the way I think about technical work. I do not want to become an IT professional who focuses only on whether something can be done. I want to ask whether it should be done and whether it is consistent with professional responsibilities.

This distinction is especially important in System Administration and Cloud Support, where administrators may have significant privileges within organizational environments.

### Connection to My Faith and Professional Values

My faith also influences how I approach ethical decisions. Christlike principles such as honesty, integrity, compassion, humility, and respect for others provide a foundation for how I want to conduct myself professionally.

Integrity means that I should not misuse access simply because I have the technical ability to do so. Compassion reminds me that decisions involving personal information can affect people in ways that may not be immediately visible. Humility reminds me that I may not always know every policy or circumstance and should be willing to seek guidance.

The ethical dilemma therefore connects closely with the three principles that have become central to my professional growth portfolio: integrity, service, and continuous learning.

**Integrity** guides my decisions.

**Service** reminds me that technology should be used to help rather than harm people.

**Continuous learning** reminds me to keep developing my understanding of privacy, cybersecurity, professional standards, and ethical responsibilities.

My cloud computing experiences have reinforced this same principle. Managing users, groups, storage, virtual machines, and access permissions requires careful consideration of who should have access to information and resources. In cloud environments, security controls such as role-based access and appropriate permissions are not only technical configurations; they are part of the responsibility I have to protect organizational systems and information. This connection has strengthened my commitment to combining technical competence with integrity and ethical judgment.

### Conclusion

The ethical dilemma I examined has strengthened my understanding of the responsibilities that accompany professional access and authority. The situation demonstrated that privacy and consent should not be overlooked simply because an action appears beneficial or technically possible.As I continue preparing for a career in System Administration and Cloud Support, I want these principles to guide not only what I am technically capable of doing, but also how responsibly and ethically I choose to use those abilities.

As I prepare for a career in System Administration and Cloud Support, I understand that I will have responsibilities that extend beyond configuring systems and solving technical problems. I may have access to sensitive information and powerful administrative tools, and I will need to use that access responsibly.

In future situations, I will consider authorization, consent, privacy, organizational policies, potential harm, and professional standards before acting. If I encounter an ethical concern, I will seek clarification, communicate respectfully, and use appropriate escalation procedures rather than ignoring the problem.

Most importantly, I want my technical abilities to be guided by character. I want to be known not only as someone who can solve technical problems, but as an IT professional who can be trusted to protect information, respect people, act with integrity, and make responsible decisions.
