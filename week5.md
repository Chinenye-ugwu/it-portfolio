## Task 2B – Ethical Dilemma Reflection

### Introduction

Ethical decision-making is an important part of Information Technology because IT professionals often have access to information, systems, and resources that can significantly affect other people. Technical knowledge gives a professional the ability to perform many actions, but having the ability to do something does not necessarily mean that it is appropriate to do it.

The ethical dilemma I examined earlier in my IT497 portfolio involved an insurance manager who recorded a life insurance beneficiary and shared the video publicly without clear evidence that the individual had provided informed consent. Although the situation occurred in an insurance setting rather than an IT department, it helped me recognize ethical issues that are directly relevant to my future career in Information Technology.

The situation raised important questions about privacy, consent, professional responsibility, dignity, and the appropriate use of personal information.

### My View of the Ethical Dilemma

My view is that the individual's privacy and dignity should have been respected before the recording was shared publicly. Even if the intention behind the post was positive, good intentions do not automatically remove the need for consent.

A person may be comfortable participating in an insurance transaction but may not be comfortable having their image shared publicly. These are two different decisions. Consent to participate in one activity should not automatically be interpreted as consent to use someone's image or personal information for another purpose.

I believe the manager should have obtained clear permission before recording or publicly sharing the individual's image. If permission was not provided, the safest and most respectful decision would have been not to publish the video.

This situation reinforced for me that ethical behavior requires more than asking whether an action is possible or beneficial. It requires considering how the action affects the person involved.

### Connection to Information Technology

Although the original situation was not specifically an IT problem, the principles apply directly to Information Technology.

IT professionals regularly work with information that belongs to other people. This can include usernames, passwords, employee records, customer information, financial information, system logs, photographs, communications, and other forms of personal or organizational data.

Because of this access, an IT professional may sometimes have the technical ability to view, copy, change, or share information. However, technical access does not mean that the information can be used for any purpose.

For example, a system administrator may have administrative privileges that allow access to files belonging to employees. That privilege exists to perform legitimate job responsibilities. It does not give the administrator permission to browse personal files out of curiosity or disclose confidential information.

The ethical lesson I gained from the insurance dilemma is therefore directly applicable to system administration: access must be accompanied by responsibility.

### How I Would Handle a Similar Situation in Future Work

If I encounter a similar ethical situation in my future IT career, my first response would be to identify what information or activity is involved and determine whether I have legitimate authorization to access, use, or share it.

I would consider several questions:

- Do I have authorization to access this information?
- What is the legitimate business purpose?
- Has the person provided appropriate consent when consent is required?
- Could sharing or using the information cause harm?
- Does the proposed action follow organizational policy?
- Are there legal, regulatory, security, or privacy requirements that apply?
- Would I be comfortable explaining my decision to my supervisor or another appropriate authority?

If I determined that the action was inappropriate, I would not simply proceed because someone requested it. I would respectfully raise the concern and seek clarification from the appropriate supervisor, privacy officer, security professional, or organizational authority.

If the situation involved a serious privacy or security concern, I would follow the organization's established reporting and escalation procedures.

### The Importance of Speaking Up

One of the most important lessons I take from this dilemma is that ethical responsibility sometimes requires speaking up.

It can be difficult to question an action when the person requesting it has more authority or experience. However, professionalism does not mean agreeing with every decision made by someone in a higher position.

As an IT professional, I may encounter situations in which a supervisor, coworker, or client asks me to perform an action that concerns me. I want to respond respectfully while still protecting users, organizational information, and professional standards.

I would avoid responding emotionally or accusing someone of wrongdoing without understanding the situation. Instead, I would ask questions, identify the specific concern, refer to relevant policies or procedures, and seek guidance when necessary.

This approach allows me to protect both the organization and the people affected by the decision.

### Ethical Principles I Will Carry Into My Career

Several principles from this dilemma will guide my future professional decisions.

#### Privacy

Personal information should be treated carefully. I should access and use information only when there is a legitimate reason and appropriate authorization.

#### Consent

When consent is required, I should not assume that permission for one activity automatically applies to another activity.

#### Integrity

I should do what is right even when taking the ethical path is less convenient or when no one appears to be watching.

#### Accountability

I should accept responsibility for my actions and be willing to explain the decisions I make.

#### Respect for Human Dignity

Technology involves people. Behind every account, record, photograph, or data entry is a real person who deserves to be treated with respect.

#### Professional Responsibility

Technical skills should be used responsibly. An IT professional's ability to access a system creates an obligation to protect that system and the people whose information it contains.

### What This Experience Taught Me

This ethical dilemma helped me understand that ethical challenges in IT are not always obvious. They may appear as ordinary technical requests.

For example, someone might ask an administrator to retrieve information from an employee's account, provide access to a file, disable a security control, share a screenshot, or make a system change. The request may sound simple, but each situation can involve questions about authorization, privacy, security, and responsibility.

The experience has therefore changed the way I think about technical work. I do not want to become an IT professional who focuses only on whether something can be done. I want to ask whether it should be done and whether it is consistent with professional responsibilities.

This distinction is especially important in System Administration and Cloud Support, where administrators may have significant privileges within organizational environments.

### Connection to My Faith and Professional Values

My faith also influences how I approach ethical decisions. Christlike principles such as honesty, integrity, compassion, humility, and respect for others provide a foundation for how I want to conduct myself professionally.

Integrity means that I should not misuse access simply because I have the technical ability to do so. Compassion reminds me that decisions involving personal information can affect people in ways that may not be immediately visible. Humility reminds me that I may not always know every policy or circumstance and should be willing to seek guidance.

The ethical dilemma therefore connects closely with the three principles that have become central to my professional growth portfolio: integrity, service, and continuous learning.

**Integrity** guides my decisions.

**Service** reminds me that technology should be used to help rather than harm people.

**Continuous learning** reminds me to keep developing my understanding of privacy, cybersecurity, professional standards, and ethical responsibilities.

### Conclusion

The ethical dilemma I examined has strengthened my understanding of the responsibilities that accompany professional access and authority. The situation demonstrated that privacy and consent should not be overlooked simply because an action appears beneficial or technically possible.

As I prepare for a career in System Administration and Cloud Support, I understand that I will have responsibilities that extend beyond configuring systems and solving technical problems. I may have access to sensitive information and powerful administrative tools, and I will need to use that access responsibly.

In future situations, I will consider authorization, consent, privacy, organizational policies, potential harm, and professional standards before acting. If I encounter an ethical concern, I will seek clarification, communicate respectfully, and use appropriate escalation procedures rather than ignoring the problem.

Most importantly, I want my technical abilities to be guided by character. I want to be known not only as someone who can solve technical problems, but as an IT professional who can be trusted to protect information, respect people, act with integrity, and make responsible decisions.
